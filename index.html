Aerospace Classification Rules (Active vs Debris)

These rules reflect the key physical differences detected by the Decision Tree and Random Forest models trained on the TLE database.

Feature Parameter Active Satellites (Label 0) Space Debris (Label 1) Aerospace Mechanics Justification
Eccentricity (e) Near-Circular (e < 0.001) Highly Elliptical (e > 0.01) Active payloads are circularized for stability; collision/explosion shock waves eject debris in elliptical paths.
Inclination (i) Sun-Synchronous (~98°), GEO (~0°) Clustered around collision angles (e.g. 74°, 82.6°) Active payloads occupy uniform orbits; debris remains in the exact plane of its parent object's breakup.
Altitude (Alt) Maintained at specific heights Decaying height, drifting over time Active payloads use station-keeping thrusters; debris decays continuously due to atmospheric drag.
Specific Orbital Energy Constant, controlled energy Decaying, lower energy levels Debris has no thrusters and constantly loses potential and kinetic energy to atmospheric friction.
Velocity (V) Constant speed (approx. 7.58 km/s in LEO) Varying speed (highest at perigee, lowest at apogee) Elliptical debris orbits exchange kinetic and potential energy continually along their paths.
